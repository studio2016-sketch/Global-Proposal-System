# WGOS executive attention model

WGOS follows one operating principle:

> Automate the work. Escalate the decisions.

The executive surface is not a generic task board. Normal deterministic workflows should continue without demanding executive attention. The command layer surfaces only meaningful decisions, approvals, exceptions, and opportunities.

## Attention categories
- DECISION — a judgment that cannot safely be inferred from policy.
- APPROVAL — commercial, contractual, creative, or financial authority reserved for an authorized owner.
- EXCEPTION — a workflow that cannot continue normally or has breached an expected condition.
- OPPORTUNITY — a time-sensitive or high-value situation worth executive review.

Routine tasks remain in their operational/project context. They do not become executive attention items merely because they exist.

## Safety and authority
AI may summarize and prioritize attention, but it does not grant itself authority. Commercial approval, non-catalog pricing, contract exceptions, sensitive change orders, and other configured owner-only decisions remain explicit human gates.

Attention records should reference source entities rather than duplicate their commercial truth. Neon/Postgres remains the system of record. Third-party project tools such as monday.com are optional adapters, not authoritative storage.

## Future command experience
The executive brief should answer: what is moving normally, what needs attention, why it needs attention, and the smallest safe action that resolves the blocker.
